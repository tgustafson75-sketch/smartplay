A promise about numbers

SmartPlay tells you where a number came from. A measured yardage reads differently from an estimate, and anything built from limited data is labelled as such. If the app can't know something, it says so rather than inventing it — you should be able to trust the numbers you're given precisely because you're told when not to.

2 · Playing a round

From the car park to the recap.

Start the round

  1. Open the Play tab and search for your course, or tap one of the nearby courses it's already found.
  2. Pick your round mode — Break 100 / 90 / 80, or Free Play. This changes how your caddie advises, not what it measures.
  3. Add any notes you want your caddie to know. Tap the mic icon to dictate instead of typing.
  4. Tap Start Round. You'll land on the Caddie tab with your first hole ready.
First time on a course?

SmartPlay builds the hole map for courses it hasn't seen, using public mapping data. Most courses are ready in a few seconds. A few take longer — if yours isn't ready straight away, keep playing and it will fill itself in. You don't have to do anything.

While you play

Your caddie follows along by GPS, tracks your shots and knows what hole you're on. Ask for anything you'd ask a real caddie: What have I got? What's my score? What hole am I on?

If a shot gets missed

GPS isn't perfect. When a shot doesn't register, add it by hand — it takes about ten seconds.

  1. Tools menu → Log shot.
  2. Tap ↺ Use fresh GPS fix to pin exactly where you are.
  3. Confirm the hole, then pick the club, outcome and direction.
  4. Save. Your caddie catches up immediately.

Riding rather than walking

Say Cart mode on and shot detection tightens up, so the cart's movement isn't mistaken for a shot. Saying Walking mode puts it back.

Finishing

  • Save & end — locks the round, updates your handicap, and generates your recap.
  • Discard — throws it away completely. Nothing is recorded.

If you get cut off mid-sentence

Your caddie decides you've finished when you go quiet. Trailing off softly at the end of a sentence can end the turn early. Speaking through to the end of your thought at a steady volume keeps it listening — and tapping to end the turn always works.

4 · Knowing your number

Distance is the easy part. The number that's actually useful is the one that accounts for the day.

SmartVision — see the hole

Open it from the Tools menu or say Open SmartVision. You get the hole from above, with your position live on it.

MarkerMeaning
TThe tee.
YYour target or layup. Drag it anywhere — the carry number follows.
PThe pin.
F / M / BFront, middle and back of the green.

Dragging Y is the fastest way to answer "can I get there?" — put it on the bunker and read the carry.

Plays-like distance

Raw yardage ignores the hill you're standing on and the wind in your face. Your caddie factors in elevation, conditions and how you actually hit the club — not a chart of how a tour player hits it.

TightLie — read the lie

Say Check my lie or What's the play? and then photograph the ball. You'll get an honest read on what's realistic from there — including when the answer is to take your medicine and pitch out.

SwingLab is the practice half of the app. Film a swing, get one clear thing to work on.

Filming a swing that reads well

  1. Set the phone so your whole body and the ball are in frame — the app tells you when you're framed.
  2. Film down the line (behind you, looking at the target) for path and plane. Film face on for weight and tempo.
  3. Swing. Analysis runs on its own.
Don't worry about the angle toggle

If you film down the line with the toggle set to face on, the app works out the real angle from the video and corrects itself. You won't get a wrong reading because you couldn't see the screen in bright sun.

What you get back

One primary issue — the thing worth fixing first — plus body measurements, your club's path traced through the swing, and a drill matched to what was found. You'll also see a club plane reading: whether the club came down steeper or shallower than it went back, measured from your actual clubhead, not guessed from your shoulders.

Two drills worth knowing

Transition & sequence

Step-and-Swing

Feet together. Step toward the target to start down, then swing through to a full finish over your lead leg. Forces the lower body to lead — and it ends in a real finish, which is the half most transition drills skip.

Shallowing

Pump Drill

Club to the top. Pump down to hip height three times, feeling the lower body start and the club drop behind you. On the fourth, swing through to a full finish.

6 · Your bag and your ball

Advice is only as good as knowing how far you actually hit it.

Learning your distances

Every logged shot teaches your caddie your real carry — not the chart. This matters more than it sounds: if your wedges fly further than standard and your driver doesn't, generic advice hands you the wrong club all day. Once a club has enough shots behind it, your caddie uses your number instead.

Fit Profile

Your carry ladder from longest to shortest, the gaps between clubs, and the clubs you actually own cross-referenced against those gaps — so it can tell the difference between a gap you should fill and one you already have a club for.

Ball Fit and shaft flex

Both answer from what the app can genuinely read — your speed band from a measured driver carry, your handicap, and how you use your short game.

An honest limit

SmartPlay does not measure spin, compression or launch. It says so on the screen. These reads are a sensible starting point and a good sleeve to try — they are not a launch-monitor fitting, and they'll tell you to confirm with a fitter rather than pretending otherwise.

8 · When something looks wrong

Common situations, and what's actually happening.

What you seeWhat to do
Yardage says STATICThe hole map is still building. Keep playing — it switches to live yardage on its own once the greens land.
Course won't loadGive it a minute. If the map is slow to build, the app asks again by itself. If a course never builds, tell your caddie and play from the scorecard yardages.
An estimated hole mapWhen a hole is marked as an estimate, treat it as a guide rather than a surveyed number. It's labelled precisely so you know which is which.
Caddie cut you offSpeak through to the end of your sentence, or tap to end the turn yourself.
A shot didn't registerTools → Log shot → fresh GPS fix. Ten seconds and your caddie is caught up.
Something's plainly brokenSay Log this — [what's wrong] and it is saved with the context of what you were doing.

What SmartPlay doesn't claim

Being useful matters more than sounding certain. The app works with a phone, a watch, your camera and public data — not radar. So it measures what it can measure, estimates what it can reasonably estimate and labels it, and stays quiet about the rest. Where it says "estimated," "directional" or "still being calibrated," take it at its word — that's the app being straight with you, and it's why the unlabelled numbers are worth trusting.
